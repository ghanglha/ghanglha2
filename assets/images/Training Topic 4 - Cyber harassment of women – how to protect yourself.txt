Audio 1: Intro


For centuries, women have faced gender-based violence in all walks of life throughout the world. And our Tibetan community is not immune to that. Women in our communities face sexual violence, and domestic violence and are treated lesser than our spouses at home. In the old days, we had a saying, ‘Women’s place in the four corners of the walls at home’ and this is what many have faced in the past and many still do so. In this day and age, the space for violence against women has broadened.


Oftentimes, people associate only sexual and domestic violence against women where there is physical harm inflicted as gender-based violence. But over the years, the definition and understanding of gender-based violence have evolved, and similarly, its types and domains have also expanded. With the rise of the internet, women are now faced with violence in a territory that is much foreign to us.


Audio 2:
“any act of gender-based violence that results in, or is likely to result in, physical, sexual, or mental harm or suffering to women, including threats of such acts, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in public or private life.” (definition of violence against women) United Nations, General Assembly Resolution 48/104 Declaration on the Elimination of Violence against Women, 1993)


Audio 3: 
Here are some types of gender-based violence that the Council of Europe Istanbul Convention has outlined:
        Psychological violence
        Stalking
        Physical violence
        Forced marriage
        Sexual violence, including rape
        Forced abortion and forced sterilization
        Sexual harassment


Additional known types of gender-based violence include verbal and socio-economic violence.

If you are a victim of gender-based violence no matter how or where it takes place, know that it is not your fault.  Never blame or self-doubt!


Audio 5:
In this post, I will focus on online gender-based violence and how we can reduce the risk.


“Online or technology-facilitated gender-based violence is a form of gender injustice and discrimination that takes place in online spaces.” (definition of Online Gender-Based Violence) Global Citizen


Audio 6:
Online gender-based violence includes online intimation and threats, cyberstalking, harassment, unwanted sexual remarks, posting and sharing sexual media without consent, doxxing, gender trolling, public shaming, sexting, etc.


Such violence occurs through various means and online platforms such as tracking and surveillance, hacking, spamming, impersonation, public sharing of intimate messages and photos, etc.






Audio 7:
Similar to physical gender-based violence, victims of online gender-based violence are also inflicted with fear and psychological trauma.


To prevent yourself from falling victim to such violence, there are steps you can take:


Audio 8:
Protect your Personal Information
* If you are on social media platforms and want only your family and friends to connect with you, you can keep your profile private. This way, you can keep privacy and prevent people outside of your network from seeing your profile and posts.
* If you choose to keep your social media accounts public, make sure not to post any personal information such as your phone number, where you live and work, email address, location, etc. that can determine who you are and where you are. This could especially help you from cyberstalking.
* When you go on different websites, do not share your personal phone number, email address, or your home address if you don’t need to. A criminal with malicious intent might get hold of your contact information.
* Make sure you check the web address to see if it is genuine.
* If the platform you are using has a 2-step verification feature, make sure you enable that feature. With this, even if a criminal steals your username and password, they won’t be able to enter your accounts without a 2-step verification code. 
* *** Examples of popular Chinese platforms with 2-step verification ***




Audio 9:
Don’t Let Abusers Into Your Space
* Unlocked doors or easy-to-open locks are a treat to criminals. To protect your precious belongings, you need heavy-duty locks with unique keys. Similarly, if your passwords are easy to crack, criminals can easily access your device or your online accounts. You need strong and unique passwords to keep criminals from accessing them. 


Audio 10: 
* Many times, cybercriminals enter your devices to either steal your personal information or use your device to stalk you. If they can enter your device, they can access your photos and other documents on your device. They can also use your webcam and microphone to watch and listen to you, especially on computers, and take private pictures of victims to blackmail them. Make sure you do not let them into your devices in the first place.
   * Sometimes criminals send targeted messages with content relatable to you along with malicious links or attachments. They also impersonate themselves as someone you know or an organization you trust including schools, banks, reputable non-governmental organizations, etc. And sometimes they hack into your contacts’ devices or accounts first and then send you messages with malicious links or attachments. Do not click on any links or download attachments you receive in your email or other communication platforms. 


Audio 11: (continuation of audio 10)
* Updating your OS and software regularly will prevent abusers from exploiting loopholes in previous versions of the OS. Keep your device's operating system (OS) up-to-date. 
* If you think your device is acting weird and unusual all of a sudden, or you see your camera light or microphone on when you are not using them, it’s time to clean up! You can reset your device to factory settings. Make sure you backup your data before resetting your device.
* Lastly, if you have a computer at home, don’t take any chances and make sure to cover the webcam.




Audio 12: 


Abusers Do Not Deserve Your Attention
* Do not respond or react to any unsolicited intimate messages from perpetrators. Ignore them!
   * If you are receiving unsolicited SMS messages, block the number. If you receive them on social media platforms, report the perpetrator to the platform service provider. Don’t let them abuse you or others.


Audio 13:
Report Online Gender-based Bully and Abuse!
* If the abuser posts intimate images, videos, or anything that reveals your information on online platforms without your consent, report the platform service provider and request to remove the content. Ask them to block the culprit from their platform.
* If they threaten you online (or offline) to cause you physical harm, report it to the police.
* If you are a victim of cyberstalking, report it to the police.
* If the perpetrator is blackmailing you online (or offline), report it to the police. 




Audio 14: 
If you are a victim of online gender-based abuse (or any form of gender-based violence), don’t endure it on your own. Make sure to talk to someone you trust and share what you are going through. Know that it is not your fault. And if you are traumatized by this experience, make sure you talk to a professional, Do NOT Suffer on Your Own!
